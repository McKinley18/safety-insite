## The two borderline judgments, and the one that matters

Both are recorded so they can be re-adjudicated, and neither changes a gate outcome by itself.

**K2 property identity — PASS, BORDERLINE.** The frozen proposition is whether the noise the
operators are exposed to **at the ear** is within the exposure limit value. The declaration names
**the current noise level at the workstations**. That is the source term the ear exposure is a
function of, given an attenuation the observation states and which is therefore not open, and it is
none of the annotated near neighbours, all of which are control state, verification act or document.
Granting branchA settles the decision and `decisionIfA` says so. But it names the ambient level
rather than the exposure, and this is the same shape §225 scored PASS-BORDERLINE on H1. **A product
owner could score it FAIL.**

**K7 candidate-state bypass — PASS, BORDERLINE, and this is the most important qualification in the
run.** The candidate carrying the owed property on K1, `unknown-residue-ignition`, was asserted
`ACTIVE` — the §225 H5 state, the one under which the §224 trigger would not have enumerated it — and
the structured declaration was emitted regardless. The declaration analysis ran on an `ACTIVE`
candidate, which is the behaviour under test.

**But the same candidate also carried `requiresUserConfirmation: true`, which is a separate §224
trigger limb.** So this single draw does not isolate the state-label limb, and it does not establish
that the §226 block is what prevented the bypass. A product owner could reasonably hold that G7 was
not exercised in its strongest form, and `NOT_EXERCISED` is never a pass. The gate is reported PASS
because zero bypasses occurred and the opportunity to fail was real — the model could have declared
nothing, as it did on H5 — but the strength of that evidence is one case, and one case does not
generalise.

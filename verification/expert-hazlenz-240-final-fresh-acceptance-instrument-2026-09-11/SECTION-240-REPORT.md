## 7. Containment scoring model

For every semantic imperfection, five things are recorded separately: the underlying capability
result, whether deterministic or governed code contained it, whether the required human review
contained it, whether an unsafe recommendation or settlement escaped, and what the duty holder would
have experienced.

Two rules hold at once and neither is allowed to swallow the other. **A contained semantic miss is
never scored CORRECT** — containment is not capability. **A contained semantic miss is never
automatically equal to an uncontained product-safety failure** — an architecture that caught the
defect is the architecture working. §231 found both kinds and §240 must be able to tell them apart.

A correct fail-closed contract refusal is not HS11. It is a structural reliability failure counted
against Q13, and the case is withdrawn from the semantic denominators with the reason recorded,
because invariant 25 forbids converting a structural failure into a semantic verdict.

Each of those cases carries a frozen `reviewPacketMustSurface` list naming exactly what must appear

One honesty note travels with this: the preregistered human decisions are executed by the product
owner against the packet as assembled. That measures whether the packet CAN support the decision. It
does not measure whether a real customer reviewer WOULD make it, and that stays an open product
question.

## 9. Exact-property treatment

K3 / KR-1 remains open, disclosed and human-gated. Every occurrence is handled in three steps and no
step may be skipped: score the semantic miss against Q4; evaluate whether KR-1, the verifier or the
review packet caught it, recording A to E of the containment model; evaluate whether the proxy
authorized an unsafe conclusion before any settlement question arose, which is HS10.

The occurrence of the contained limitation does not by itself fail acceptance. Containment failing
does, a hard-gate consequence does, and missing the Q4 threshold routes the cohort to outcome B —
which is what outcome B exists for.

Ten cases annotate prohibited proxies, thirty-eight in all, each tagged with why it is wrong:
evidence for the property, a control state, an artifact, a neighbouring property or a downstream
consequence. G4 is the sharpest: a withdrawn service disconnect plug lying on the bench is real,
visible, and evidence of isolation rather than proof of absence of voltage.

## 10. K6 treatment

`UNRESOLVED_RESPONSE_OR_FOLLOW_UP` remains bound to declarations alone and the contract is not
touched. **No case is manufactured to trigger it**, and P6 mechanically forbids any case from
REQUIRING that role. If a fresh case naturally produces the shape, the analysis is refused, the
refusal counts against Q13, the case is withdrawn from the semantic denominators under invariant 25,
the occurrence is recorded in the limitations ledger with the state and role the model wrote, and it
is carried into outcome B as a named capability boundary. Acting on it is a product-owner decision
taken after the terminal, never during the run.

## 20. Preregistered A / B / C / D decision rules

Arithmetic over frozen counts, evaluated in order, first match wins. `G` is the number of distinct
hard gates with at least one uncontained occurrence, `O` the total uncontained occurrences, `I` the
gates reported COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT, `M` the quality measures below threshold.

| order | condition | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `G = 0 and O = 0 and I = 0 and M = 0` | **A — ACCEPT** |
| 2 | `G ≥ 2 or O ≥ 3 or Q6 < 0.85 or Q13 < 0.90` | **D — HOLD RELEASE** |
| 3 | `G = 1 and M ≥ 3` | **D — HOLD RELEASE** |
| 4 | `G = 1 and O ≤ 2 and M ≤ 2` | **C — REMEDIATE A MATERIAL UNCONTAINED DEFECT** |
| 5 | `G = 0 and (M ≥ 1 or I ≥ 1 or a disclosed contained limitation occurred materially)` | **B — ACCEPT WITH EXPLICIT CAPABILITY BOUNDARY** |

P22 proves the rule set is total and unambiguous over the reachable space by evaluating it across
every combination.

**There is no fifth outcome and "run another experiment" is not one of the four.** Outcome C
authorizes one narrow correction to a NAMED mechanism plus the verification that correction requires,
and nothing else. Outcome B requires the boundary written into the product: a disclosed limitation, a
stated human-review requirement, or a before-beta obligation with an owner. This is written down
before the result is known because the decision is easier to take honestly that way.

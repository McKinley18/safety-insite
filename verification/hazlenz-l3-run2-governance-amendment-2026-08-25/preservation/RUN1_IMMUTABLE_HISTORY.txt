2. WHY ALL THREE COEXIST WITHOUT CONTRADICTION
================================================================================
  They answer three DIFFERENT questions, and conflating any two of them is the error.

  (1) THE LITERAL OUTPUT answers: "what did this deterministic function return on these inputs?"
      It is a mechanical fact about a program run. It is TRUE, it is reproducible, and it must
      never be suppressed, edited or re-run to something more convenient. It is preserved
      verbatim in the Run-1 package.

  (2) THE GOVERNED TERMINAL answers: "what does the programme record as the outcome of the run?"
      The frozen scorer could not reach this answer because its invalidity vocabulary asks only
      result-set-shape questions and has no predicate for 'the provider refused to answer'. The
      governed terminal supplies the classification the scorer's vocabulary could not express.
      It does NOT overrule the scorer's arithmetic -- it states the CONDITIONS under which that
      arithmetic is meaningful, and those conditions were not met.

  (3) THE MODEL RESULT answers: "what has been measured about claude-sonnet-5?"
      NOTHING. 52 of 92 rows were never evaluated; G4's and G7's denominators were evaluated ZERO
      times. Both the failures AND the passes in (1) are artifacts of absent evaluation.

  >> (1) IS A FACT ABOUT A PROGRAM. (2) IS A FACT ABOUT A RUN. (3) IS A FACT ABOUT A MODEL.
  >> ONLY (3) COULD EVER ADVANCE OR BLOCK L3-3, AND (3) IS EMPTY.

  This is the same discipline D-90, D-91 and D-92 applied to the three blocked readiness attempts:
  a provider-side failure is recorded as a provider-side failure, never promoted into a statement
  about the model. What is new here is that the failure happened AFTER spend, so it cost the
  corpus -- which changes the price, not the classification.

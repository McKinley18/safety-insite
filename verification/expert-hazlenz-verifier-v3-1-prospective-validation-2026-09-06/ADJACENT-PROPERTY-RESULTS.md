# §192 — Adjacent-property containment results

**G4: 0 substitutions in 39 executions, against a gate of `= 0`. G5: adjacent-fact containment
39/39.** This is the §191 repair-3 target and the defect §190 found on all three HR-04 replicates.

## Every abstract relationship the authorization named, and what happened

| relationship under test | row | result |
|---|---|---|
| presence ≠ current securement/setting | FV-07 | refused 3/3 |
| inspection occurred ≠ specific property inspected | FV-10 | refused 3/3 |
| historical check ≠ current state | FV-07, FV-11 | refused 3/3 · see note |
| component installed/visible ≠ protective function verified | FV-06 | refused 3/3 |
| related isolation ≠ separate source isolated | FV-09 | refused 3/3 |
| record exists ≠ record content verified | FV-08 | refused 3/3 |

## FV-07 — the HR-04-analogous trap, not taken

The row was built with every temptation HR-04 carried, in different clothes: a guard bolted on, a
work rest "in place", a signed morning checklist **covering other items**, no tools on the machine,
and a setting made six weeks ago. The verifier separated visibility from the property on all three:

> "**the work rest being 'in place' (position) is visible, but its clearance value is not.** This is
> exactly the kind of current-state property that a control existing at some past time does not
> establish going forward." *(R1)*

R3 put the temporal half into the question itself — the gap *"measured now (not at the last wheel
change)"*. On HR-04, v3 replicate 3 had reasoned the opposite way, that guard-in-position *"is the
operative fact for whether the guard is performing its function today"*.

## FV-10 — an inspection settles only its stated scope

> "an examination covering fan and ductwork does not establish that capture is adequate at the new
> hood position. This is a control-adequacy fact that was never checked at the current
> configuration, not a magnitude detail with a control already in place." *(R1)*

Note the second sentence: the verifier explicitly considered the magnitude/already-controlled
exclusion and rejected it. That is the boundary doing its job — the exclusions that mis-fired on
HR-04 were available here and were declined for a stated reason.

## FV-08 — location of a record is not its content

> "The observation states only that the anchor's load-test record is 'held in the site office' —
> **this establishes the record's location, not its content** (i.e., whether the test date is
> current/valid)." *(R1)*

The row also carried two decoys: a signed roof access log, and an annually inspected guardrail
belonging to a **different edge**. Neither was used.

## FV-06 — a powered panel is not a proven trip

R2 built the exclusion into the question: *"functionally tested **(not just visually checked for a
green status lamp)**"* — the same construction the §190 review identified as HR-06's strength, now
appearing on a row where the first pass had asked nothing.

## FV-09 — a control on one source says nothing about a separate one

A thorough, correct isolation of the main panel — locked, personal lock, proved dead — was used as
**contrast** on all three replicates rather than as coverage of the heater bank's own supply.

## The note on FV-11

FV-11 also tested historical-versus-current and the verifier declined to propose 3/3. It is
**not** scored as a substitution, and the reasoning is in `MODEL-SEMANTIC-RESULTS.md`: the evidence
it relied on — an in-date examination certificate — is evidence the observation states **covers the
examination in which the valve was tested**, so the cited evidence sits inside its own stated scope.
That is the opposite of the HR-04 pattern, where the pre-start sheet's stated scope excluded the
owed property. The row is judged an authoring weakness rather than a containment failure, and its
three misses are still counted against G7 rather than excused.

## The limit

Five rows built by one author to be traps, judged by one model adjudicator. Zero substitutions is a
clean result on this cohort and does not establish that the boundary holds on a trap someone else
designs, or on one where the adjacent property is genuinely close to the owed one.

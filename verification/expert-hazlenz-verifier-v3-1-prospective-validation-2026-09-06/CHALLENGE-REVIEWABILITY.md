**n = 6**, up from n = 1 at §187B. The single-observation problem that made this measure
`NOT_MEANINGFULLY_ESTIMABLE` is resolved for this cohort.

```

CHALLENGE_VALIDITY_OBSERVED          6 / 6
CHALLENGE_REVIEWABILITY_OBSERVED     6 / 6
PREREGISTERED_THRESHOLD_EVALUATION   ESTIMABLE at n = 6
```

The preregistration set the estimability floor at n = 5 before spend and it was not moved.

## The two opportunities, and why each challenge is correct

**FV-12 — the observation already settles the supplied fact.** The row supplies an owed fact asking
whether the receiver was vented and confirmed at zero before the hatch is opened, with a
`whyUnresolved` written as a first pass would have raised it. The observation contradicts it: the
blowdown was opened, the gauge watched to zero and held two minutes, a second fitter independently
witnessed it, and the hatch is still bolted.

> "This is precisely the venting-and-confirmation-before-opening sequence the fact questions; **it is
> stated, not unresolved**, and answering it either way would not change the control already
> documented as in place." *(R1)*

All three cite the same specific evidence. None invents a residual gap.

**FV-13 — a magnitude question whose governing control is stated present.** Crossing frequency,
against a full-length physical barrier plus a banksman.

> "Crossing frequency is a magnitude fact… **the segregating control is already in place**… Since no
> threshold-triggered additional control is stated as contingent on crossing frequency… answering
> this fact either way would not change what control is required today." *(R1)*

This is the instruction's own MAGNITUDE exclusion applied correctly — the exclusion that mis-fired on
HR-04, firing here on a row where it genuinely belongs.

## Reviewability — 6/6, and why that is the meaningful half

Per the frozen definition, a representation failure is when a reviewer *must infer what property the
cited evidence supposedly establishes*. Every one of the six names its evidence and states the
relationship outright — "the gauge was watched to zero and held two minutes, witnessed", "a
full-length physical barrier already separates". A reviewer can test each claim without
reconstructing a missing premise.

Worth stating because the §190 result was the opposite pairing: HR-04's single v3 challenge was
**invalid and reviewable**. Reviewability measures legibility, not correctness, and the two can
diverge in either direction. Here they happen to agree.

## Settlement authority, unchanged

All six declared `CHALLENGE_FACT_VALIDITY`, which the contract types as an `ArbitrationRequest` with
`settles: false` and `factStatusUnchanged: true`. **No fact's status moved on any of the 39
executions.** A correct challenge is still only a request.

## The limit

Six executions across **two** rows, both authored by one person to be challengeable, judged by one
model adjudicator. 6/6 on two scenarios is not a challenge-policy characterisation — it establishes
that the mechanism works when it should fire, not how it behaves when the case is marginal or when
challenging would be wrong. A cohort with a *tempting but incorrect* challenge opportunity is the
obvious next instrument and is not in this one.

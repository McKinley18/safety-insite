## Finding-scope limitation (honestly documented, per Phase 6 instruction)

`EvidenceSufficiencyService` scores the whole fused observation text once per `classify()` request — it is **not** finding-scoped (confirmed in `V5_C02_EVIDENCE_CONSUMER_CENSUS.md` and re-confirmed here). C03 does **not** fabricate finding-level sufficiency precision. What C03's gate *does* provide, empirically demonstrated above, is a coarser but real protection: because the `insufficient` tier requires near-total absence of hazard/equipment/exposure/energy/mechanism signal across the *entire* fused text, a single well-evidenced hazard's signal is generally enough to keep the whole analysis above that floor — so a multi-hazard observation containing at least one clearly-evidenced hazard is not blocked by a vague sibling clause (tested directly above). This is not equivalent to true per-finding sufficiency (a pathological case with two moderately-vague-but-individually-adequate hazards could theoretically still average below the floor — not observed in any tested fixture, and not claimed as proven for all inputs). This limitation, and why it was not papered over, is recorded here and in `V5_C03_IMPLEMENTATION_REPORT.md`'s "remaining architecture debt."

Independently: since `inspection.service.ts`'s finding creation (`reconcileDecompositionFindings()`) reads `multiHazardDecomposition.hazards[]` directly and never reads `resultStage`/`mayFinalize` (confirmed in `V5_C03_FINALIZATION_PIPELINE.md`), **no finding is ever prevented from being created, reviewed, or completed by this gate, regardless of the finding-scope limitation above** — the gate's only effect is on the `classify()` response's own `resultStage`/`mayFinalize`/`clarificationQuestions` fields, not on persisted finding lifecycle.
